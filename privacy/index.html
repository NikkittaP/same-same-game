Privacy Policy — Same Same!

Last updated: 2026-11-14
Applies to the iPhone and iPad app and to the Android app, both com.nikitapetrovapps.samesame. Where the two differ, it is said below.

Summary

Same Same! collects no data at all. The developer runs no servers for the game, and the game sends nothing to anyone: it shows no advertising, contains no analytics, does no tracking and asks for no account. Everything the game remembers stays in its own private folder on the device.

The app plays fully offline. The only time anything goes over the network is when the store itself is involved: the App Store on iPhone and iPad, or Google Play on Android, processes the one-time in-app purchase (and, on Android, offers app updates). That goes through the store on the device, not through the developer (see Purchases and updates).

What the game stores on the device

  • which difficulty step the child is on, and which world was open last time;
  • how many pairs have been found with each animal — this is what fills the album;
  • how the child dressed the animals in the album;
  • counters of rounds, missed taps and playing time per day, used to draw the chart in the parents' menu;
  • the interface language, whether sound is on, and the age setting that adjusts how forgiving taps are;
  • whether the full version is unlocked on this device, and (on Android) which offered app update an adult has chosen to skip.

These records contain no name, age, photo, voice, location or device identifier — nothing that points at a person. The game does not sync them to iCloud or any other cloud. They are removed together with the app, and also by Start over in the parents' menu.

What the game does NOT collect

  • No personal data: no name, e-mail, phone number, address or date of birth.
  • No accounts, no sign-in, no cloud saves.
  • No analytics, telemetry, crash reporting, trackers or advertising identifiers. The app does not track anyone across other companies' apps or websites, so it never shows Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt — it has nothing to ask.
  • No advertising and no ad networks.
  • No third-party SDKs that collect data, and no data sharing — there is no data to share. The only code that talks to anything outside the app is the store's own: Apple's StoreKit on iPhone and iPad, Google's Play Billing and Play in-app updates libraries on Android. They talk only to the App Store or the Google Play app on the device.
  • No chat, comments, user-generated content or any other communication between people.

Permissions

iPhone and iPad. The app asks for no permissions at all — no camera, microphone, photos, location, contacts, Bluetooth, notifications or tracking. Its privacy manifest declares no tracking.

Android. The app requests no runtime permissions — no camera, microphone, location, contacts or file access. It declares three install-time permissions that are needed only by Google Play: INTERNET and ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE (so Google Play can reach its servers for a purchase or an update check) and com.android.vending.BILLING (so the game can ask Google Play whether the full version is owned).

On both platforms the game itself opens no network connections of its own.

Links out of the app

The parents' menu — reached only after solving a simple arithmetic problem, so a child does not open it by accident — has a page listing the research the game is built on, and an About & Credits page naming the developer and the illustrator who worked on some of the game's artwork. Tapping a source, the illustrator's portfolio link or the developer's website link opens that website in the device browser. The portfolio and website links carry a tag saying they came from the game (utm_source=samesame) and nothing else — nothing about the device, the child or how they play. Those websites have their own privacy policies; for the developer's website, see This website.

Apart from those two pages, the game contains no outbound links, and there are none anywhere in the part of the game a child plays.

Children

The game is made for children roughly between one and five years old. It is designed to meet the App Store rules for the Kids Category, the Google Play Families policy, the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) and the GDPR rules on children's data. Since the game collects nothing and stores nothing outside the device, there is no personal data of a child to protect, disclose or delete on request.

The game speaks to an adult in the parents' menu behind the arithmetic gate, and nothing from there leaves the device. Purchases and links out of the app are only there: a child playing rounds or browsing the album never sees an offer to buy anything or a link to leave the game.

Purchases and updates

The little town world is free and complete. The other worlds are unlocked by a single one-time in-app purchase, the full version, with no subscription.

iPhone and iPad. The payment is handled entirely by Apple through the App Store under Apple's privacy policy. The developer neither receives nor stores card numbers, billing addresses, the Apple Account or any other payment details. The game only asks the App Store whether this Apple Account owns the full version and remembers the answer on the device; Restore Purchases in the parents' menu asks again. The purchase can be shared through Family Sharing, and with Ask to Buy the request goes to the parent in Apple's own interface — the game takes no part in it. App updates are delivered by the App Store itself; the game does not check for them.

Android. The payment is handled entirely by Google Play under Google's privacy policy: the developer neither receives nor stores card numbers, billing addresses, the Google account or any other payment details. The game only asks Google Play whether this account owns the full version and remembers the answer on the device. When the app starts, it asks Google Play whether a newer version is available. If there is one, a small note offers to update; it can be dismissed, and a dismissed version is not offered again. That check is carried out by Google Play on the device, and nothing about the child or how they play is part of it.

This website

This website, unlike the app, counts visits with Umami: which pages are opened, from which site a visitor came, and the browser, device type and country. Umami sets no cookies and does not identify or follow individual visitors. None of this is part of the app, and nothing a child does in the game ever reaches this website.
